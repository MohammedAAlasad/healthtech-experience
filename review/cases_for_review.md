## Case 3: Ulcers that keep coming back

*Case ID: `om-ras-01` · Current status: **DRAFT***

**Patient:** 20-year-old female university student, healthy

**Chief complaint (patient's words):** "I keep getting painful round ulcers inside my lips and cheeks every few months."

**History:**

- Ulcers come every 2-3 months, often during exam periods; each one heals in about 10 days without a scar.
- Currently has 2 painful ulcers on the inside of the lower lip.
- Not taking any medicines.

**Differential diagnoses** (✔ = accepted, earns points):

| # | Differential | Accepted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recurrent aphthous stomatitis (minor) | ✔ yes |
| 2 | Recurrent intraoral herpes simplex | ✔ yes |
| 3 | Traumatic ulcer | ✔ yes |

| 5 | Behcet disease | no |
| 6 | Oral squamous cell carcinoma | no |

**Investigations:**

| Investigation | Result shown to the player | Essential |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical examination of the ulcers | Two round, shallow ulcers (3-4 mm) on the lower labial mucosa with a yellow-grey base and a red halo, on non-keratinised mucosa; no vesicles; no sharp tooth or appliance nearby. | ✔ yes |
| Blood test (CBC, iron, vitamin B12, folate) | All within normal limits: no anaemia or deficiency that could cause recurrent ulcers. | ✔ yes |
| Review of systemic symptoms | No genital ulcers, eye inflammation, skin lesions, joint pain or bowel symptoms: no features of Behcet disease or other systemic disease. | ✔ yes |
| Incisional biopsy | Non-specific ulceration with inflammation. Typical small ulcers that heal within 2 weeks do not need a biopsy. | no |

**Teaching point:** Minor recurrent aphthous ulcers are small (under 10 mm), round, shallow ulcers with a red halo on non-keratinised mucosa that heal in 7-14 days without scarring. Screen for haematinic deficiency and systemic disease; biopsy any ulcer that does not heal within 2-3 weeks.

## Case 4: Pain behind the last tooth

*Case ID: `os-pericoronitis-01` · Current status: **DRAFT***

**Patient:** 22-year-old female, healthy

**Chief complaint (patient's words):** "There's pain and swelling behind my last lower tooth, and it hurts to open my mouth and swallow."

**History:**

- Started 3 days ago and getting worse; bad taste and bad breath.
- Had similar but milder pain twice last year that settled on its own.
- No recent extractions; mouth opening limited to about two fingers.

**Differential diagnoses** (✔ = accepted, earns points):

| # | Differential | Accepted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pericoronitis | ✔ yes |
| 2 | Acute apical abscess | ✔ yes |
| 3 | Periodontal abscess | ✔ yes |
| 4 | Alveolar osteitis (dry socket) | no |
| 5 | Temporomandibular disorder | no |
| 6 | Peritonsillar abscess | no |

**Investigations:**

| Investigation | Result shown to the player | Essential |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical examination of the area | 38 partially erupted with a red, swollen, tender gum flap (operculum) over it; pus on gentle pressure; the upper molar bites on the flap. 37 looks sound. | ✔ yes |
| Panoramic radiograph | Partially erupted mesioangular 38; no caries or periapical lesion on 37 or 38; roots clear of the mandibular canal. | ✔ yes |
| Temperature and lymph node check | 37.6 C; one tender, enlarged submandibular lymph node on the left; throat and tonsils look normal, uvula central. | ✔ yes |
| CBCT scan | Same tooth position as the panoramic radiograph and the roots are clear of the canal: no extra information, extra radiation. | no |
| Blood culture | No growth. A localised infection does not need blood cultures. | no |
| Biopsy of the gum flap | Inflamed gingival tissue only. The cause is clear clinically, so a biopsy adds nothing. | no |

**Final diagnosis:** Pericoronitis

**Teaching point:** Pericoronitis is infection of the gum flap (operculum) over a partially erupted tooth, usually a lower third molar. Typical signs: swollen tender operculum, pus, bad taste, trismus and pain on swallowing. Check temperature, lymph nodes and the throat for spreading infection. Management starts with irrigation under the flap and relieving trauma from the opposing tooth; antibiotics are added when there are systemic signs or spreading infection.

## Case 5: Patchy, sensitive new molars

*Case ID: `pedo-mih-01` · Current status: **DRAFT***

**Patient:** 8-year-old boy, with his mother

**Chief complaint (patient's words):** "My back teeth hurt when I brush them, and they have yellow and brown patches."

**History:**

- Mother noticed the patches soon after the first permanent molars erupted; one molar has started to chip.
- Sensitive to cold air and to brushing on those molars.

**Differential diagnoses** (✔ = accepted, earns points):

| # | Differential | Accepted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Molar-incisor hypomineralisation (MIH) | ✔ yes |
| 2 | Dental fluorosis | ✔ yes |
| 3 | Amelogenesis imperfecta | ✔ yes |
| 4 | Dental caries | no |
| 5 | Turner hypoplasia | no |
| 6 | Dentinogenesis imperfecta | no |

**Investigations:**

| Investigation | Result shown to the player | Essential |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical examination of all teeth | Sharply demarcated yellow-brown opacities on all four first permanent molars and on the upper central incisors; post-eruptive enamel breakdown on 16; primary teeth and lower incisors look normal. | ✔ yes |
| Early childhood medical history | Repeated high fevers and ear infections with several courses of antibiotics between ages 1 and 3. | ✔ yes |
| Fluoride exposure and family history | Normal fluoride toothpaste, low-fluoride drinking water, no fluoride supplements; no enamel problems in parents or siblings. | ✔ yes |
| Genetic testing | No variant found in the enamel genes tested. The pattern and negative family history had already made a genetic defect unlikely. | no |
| Blood test (CBC) | Within normal limits. It does not explain or change the diagnosis of an enamel defect. | no |
| CBCT scan | Normal developing teeth. 3D imaging gives no useful information about enamel defects and exposes a child to unnecessary radiation. | no |

**Final diagnosis:** Molar-incisor hypomineralisation (MIH)

**Teaching point:** MIH = demarcated (sharp-edged) opacities on one to four first permanent molars, often with the incisors, linked to illness in early childhood while these teeth mineralise. Fluorosis gives diffuse, symmetrical white flecks or lines after high fluoride intake; amelogenesis imperfecta affects all teeth in both dentitions and often runs in families. MIH molars are sensitive and break down, so early fluoride varnish, sealing or restoration helps.

**Chief complaint (patient's words):** "The roof of my mouth under my denture is red and feels sore and burning."

**History:**

- Has had the same upper denture for 9 years.
- Symptoms for about 2 months; no ulcers she can see; no recent change in her medicines.

**Differential diagnoses** (✔ = accepted, earns points):

| # | Differential | Accepted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Denture stomatitis (chronic erythematous candidiasis) | ✔ yes |
| 2 | Allergic contact reaction to denture acrylic | ✔ yes |
| 3 | Traumatic ulcer from the denture | no |
| 4 | Erythroplakia | ✔ yes |
| 5 | Burning mouth syndrome | no |
| 6 | Pemphigus vulgaris | no |

**Investigations:**

| Investigation | Result shown to the player | Essential |
|---|---|---|
| Clinical examination of the palate | Diffuse, smooth red area that exactly matches the outline of the denture-fitting surface; no ulcers, no white plaques; rest of the mouth normal. | ✔ yes |
| Denture hygiene and wearing history | Wears the denture day and night and only rinses it with water; visible plaque on the fitting surface. | ✔ yes |

**Teaching point:** Denture stomatitis is redness of the mucosa under a denture, linked to Candida, poor denture hygiene, wearing the denture at night, an old ill-fitting denture and diabetes. A red patch that does not follow the denture outline, or does not resolve, needs a biopsy to exclude erythroplakia.

## Case 8: Swollen face, tooth feels high

*Case ID: `endo-aaa-01` · Current status: **DRAFT***

**Chief complaint (patient's words):** "My face is swollen on the right side and my lower tooth feels too high when I bite."

**History:**

- Swelling started yesterday; throbbing pain, worse on biting.
- Months ago the same tooth (46) hurt a lot with hot and cold, then the pain stopped on its own.
- Large old filling on 46; temperature 37.4 C; opens her mouth and swallows normally.

**Differential diagnoses** (✔ = accepted, earns points):

| # | Differential | Accepted |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Acute apical abscess | ✔ yes |
| 2 | Symptomatic apical periodontitis | ✔ yes |
| 3 | Periodontal abscess | ✔ yes |
| 4 | Symptomatic irreversible pulpitis | no |
| 5 | Pericoronitis | no |
| 6 | Ludwig angina | no |

**Investigations:**

| Investigation | Result shown to the player | Essential |
|---|---|---|
| Cold test | 46: no response at all; neighbouring teeth respond normally. The pulp of 46 is necrotic. | ✔ yes |
| Periapical radiograph | 46: large deep restoration; widened PDL space and periapical radiolucencies at the mesial and distal root apices. | ✔ yes |
| Periodontal probing | 46: normal 2-3 mm probing depths all around, no deep pocket. A periodontal abscess is unlikely. | ✔ yes |
| Blood test (CBC) | Mildly raised white cell count, as expected with a local abscess; it does not change the diagnosis or the treatment. | no |
| MRI scan | Soft-tissue swelling of the right cheek, which the clinical examination already showed. MRI is not used for a localised dental abscess. | no |
| Biopsy of the swelling | Acute inflammation and pus only. A tooth infection needs drainage, not a biopsy. | no |

**Final diagnosis:** Acute apical abscess

**Teaching point:** Acute apical abscess (AAE): necrotic pulp (no response to cold), tender tooth, rapid swelling and pus formation, sometimes with mild fever. A history of severe pain that suddenly stopped is a classic sign of pulp death. Treat by establishing drainage (root canal access or incision) and removing the cause; antibiotics only for systemic involvement or spreading infection. Difficulty swallowing or breathing, or a raised floor of the mouth, is an emergency.
